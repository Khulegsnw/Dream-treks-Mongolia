Khar Zurkhnii Khokh Lake

Khar Zurkhnii Khukh Nuur is a scenic and historically significant destination in Khentii Province. The Combination of a peaceful lake and vast steppe landscapes allows visitors to experience Mongolia's history, nature, and nomadic heritage in one place.
According to historical accounts, in 1189, Temüjin was proclaimed Khan of the Khamag Mongol and received the title "Chinggis Khaan" in the vicinity of Khar Zurkhnii Khukh Nuur. The area is therefore closely associated with an important period in the early history of Chinggis Khaan.
Things to See and Do
    • Learn about Temüjin and the history of Chinggis Khaan
    • Enjoy the lake and surrounding steppe landscapes
    • Take photographs of the peaceful natural scenery
    • Experience local traditions and nomadic culture
    • Relax in the quiet countryside

    The Hearth of Chinggis Khan

    The Hearth of Chinggis Khan is a historical and archaeological site located in Öndör Denj, Sogoot bag of Tsenkhermandal soum, Khentii Province. It is approximately 50 km north of the soum center and about 3 km from Tavan Tolgoi Hill.
    The monument consists of four flat stone slabs arranged in a box-like structure, decorated with carved patterns. The front slab features two-eight-petaled flower motifs.Although locally known as "The Hearth of Chinggis Khan," archaeological research suggests that the structure may have been a ritual site dating to the Turkic period, predating the era of Chinggis Khaan.
    Highlights
    - Explore the ancient stone structure and carvings
    - See the distinctive eight-petaled flower motifs
    - Discover Turkic-period archaeological heritage
    - Enjoy the landscapes around Tavan Tolgoi and Öndör Denj
    - Take photographs of the historic site and surrounding steppe

    Baldan Bereeven Monastery

    Baldan Bereeven Monastery is an important historical, religious, and cultural heritage site in Ömnödelger soum, Khentii Province. Located at the foot Munkh-Ulzii Mountain, the monastery is surrounded by forests, mountains, granite cliffs, and peaceful landscapes. Established in the late 18th century, the monastery became a major center of buddhism, education, and culture in eastern Mongolia during the 19th century. At its height, it was home to thousands of monks and numerous temples and religious buildings.
    The surrounding cliffs feature buddhist images, religious inscriptions, mantras, and the Soyombo symbol, making the site particularly distinctive. Much of the monastery was destroyed during the political and religious repression of the 1930s, but its ruins, rock carvings, and stone structures continue to preserve its history.
    Things to See and Do
    • Explore the monastery ruins and restored structures
    • Discover Buddhist rock carvings, inscriptions, and the Soyombo symbol
    • Learn about Mongolian Buddhism and monastic history
    • Hike through the surrounding mountains, forests, and cliffs
    • Photograph the monastery, rock carvings, and natural scenery
    • Experience the peaceful atmosphere of this historic site

    Hustai National Park

    Hustai National Park is a remarkable natural destination west of Ulaanbaatar and one of the best places to see the Przewalski's horse, or takhi, in its natural envionment.

    The Hustai Mountains combine hills, valleys, open steppe, streams, and woodland, providing an important habitat for diverse wildlife and bird species. The park is internationally known for the successful reintroduction of the takhi, which had disappeared from the wild.

    Today, herds of takhi roam freely across the park, offering visitors a rare opportunity to observe these wild horses in their natural habitat.
    Things to See and Do
    • Watch takhi and other wild animals
    • Go birdwatching and wildlife photography
    • Learn about takhi and conservation
    • Hike through the Hustai Mountains
    • Discover Mongolia’s steppe ecosystem and biodiversity
    • Enjoy beautiful sunrise and sunset landscapes
    • Join guided wildlife-watching excursions
